Module 01 — Foundation
How Off-Grid Solar Works
Before buying anything, understand how the system works as a whole. Every component has one specific job — and they only function correctly when connected in the right order.
What "Off-Grid" Means

An off-grid solar system generates and stores its own electricity — completely independent from the utility grid. It relies on solar panels to capture sunlight, batteries to store energy for nighttime or cloudy days, and a set of control components to keep everything balanced and safe. There are no monthly electric bills, but also no grid as a backup. Your system needs to be sized correctly from the start.

The 4 Core Components
🔆

Solar Panel

Converts sunlight into DC electricity using the photovoltaic effect. Rated in watts, sized to meet your daily energy needs.

⚙️

Charge Controller

Sits between the panels and the battery. Prevents overcharging and deep discharge, which would damage the battery.

🔋

Battery Bank

Stores energy produced during the day to power your home at night. The most expensive part of the system.

🔌

Inverter

Converts DC power from the battery into AC power — compatible with standard appliances like fridges, TVs, and laptops.

Key insight: Every component must be sized to match the others. A large solar array paired with an undersized battery wastes energy. The modules ahead walk you through sizing each piece correctly — in the right order.

Module 02 — First Step
Know Your Energy Needs
Before choosing any component, you need to know how much electricity you use per day. This single number drives every calculation that follows.
How to Find Appliance Wattage

Every appliance has a power label (nameplate) on the back or bottom. Look for the number followed by "W" — that's the wattage. Multiply it by the hours per day you use the appliance to get its daily energy in watt-hours (Wh). For a more accurate reading, use a plug-in wattmeter between the appliance and the outlet.

Module 03 — Storage
Choosing Your Battery Bank
The battery bank is the heart of your system — and its biggest cost. Getting the sizing right means you'll never run out of power, and the batteries will last for years.
Battery Types
LiFePO4 ✓ Best
AGM / Sealed
Flooded Lead-Acid

Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) is the best choice for most residential systems. It lasts 2,000–5,000 cycles, can be discharged to 80% safely, requires zero maintenance, and needs no ventilation. The higher upfront cost is offset by its much longer lifespan — over 10 years of daily cycling.

AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) batteries are sealed, maintenance-free, and spill-proof. Limit discharge to 50% DOD to maximize lifespan. Good for vacation homes where regular maintenance isn't possible.

Flooded Lead-Acid (FLA) batteries are the cheapest option but require monthly maintenance — checking water levels every 1–3 months. They release hydrogen gas and need ventilation. Best for full-time off-gridders comfortable with hands-on upkeep.

Module 04 — Generation
Sizing Your Solar Panels
Your panels need to produce enough energy each day to power your home and recharge your batteries. The key variable is how many peak sun hours your location receives.
Panel Types
⬛

Monocrystalline ✓ Best

17–22% efficiency. Single silicon crystal. Best for limited roof space. Higher cost but longest lifespan. Recommended for most homes.

🔷

Polycrystalline

15–17% efficiency. Multiple silicon crystals. Lower cost, slightly larger footprint. Good budget option for homes with ample roof area.

What Are Peak Sun Hours?

Peak Sun Hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day when sunlight averages 1,000 W/m². It's not the same as daylight hours — a location may have 12 hours of daylight but only 5 peak sun hours. Use globalsolaratlas.info to find your exact value. Most locations range from 3.5 to 6.5 hours. Always use a slightly conservative estimate to size for worst-case months.

Module 06 — Conversion
Inverters
The inverter converts DC battery power into AC power for your home appliances. Choosing the wrong type can damage sensitive electronics over time.
Inverter Types
〰️

Pure Sine Wave ✓ Required

Produces clean AC identical to grid power. Works with all appliances — fridges, motors, medical equipment, sensitive electronics. Always choose this type.

⊓⊔

Modified Sine Wave ✗ Avoid

Cheaper but produces a stepped waveform that damages motors, fridges, microwave ovens, and sensitive electronics over time. Not recommended.

Module 07 — Connections
Wiring, Cables & Safety
Undersized cables and missing fuses are the leading cause of off-grid system fires. This module ensures every wire and protective device is correctly rated for your system.
Where Fuses Must Be Installed

NEC requires fuses or breakers at three locations: (1) between solar panels and charge controller, (2) between charge controller and battery bank, and (3) between battery bank and inverter. A fourth breaker at the inverter AC output is also strongly recommended.

⚠️

Safety rule: Always connect the battery last and disconnect it first. Reverse polarity on DC systems can instantly destroy your charge controller and inverter. Use red for positive (+) and black for negative (−) throughout.

Module 08 — Build
Installation & Wiring Order
Never connect components in random order. Follow this checklist strictly — connecting the battery first or with wrong polarity can destroy your entire system instantly.
Correct Wiring Sequence

Always wire your system in this order: 1. Mount panels (do not connect yet) → 2. Mount and wire charge controller → 3. Connect battery to controller → 4. Connect panels to controller → 5. Connect inverter to battery → 6. Connect loads. Reverse this order when disconnecting for maintenance.

Module 09 — Operations
Monitoring & Maintenance
A well-maintained system lasts decades. Knowing what to watch for keeps your batteries healthy and your home powered even during extended cloudy stretches.
What to Monitor
🌡️

Battery Temperature

A temperature sensor connected to your charge controller automatically adjusts charge voltage to compensate for heat or cold — extending battery life significantly.

⚡

State of Charge (SOC)

Track your battery percentage daily. If it rarely reaches 80% or frequently drops below your DOD limit, your system may be undersized for your actual usage.

📡

Remote Monitoring

Most quality MPPT controllers support WiFi adapters (like eBox-WiFi) and remote meters, letting you monitor production and battery status from your phone in real time.

🌫️

Panel Cleanliness

Dust and bird droppings can reduce panel output by 15–25%. Check and clean monthly in dry climates, quarterly in wet areas. Use a soft cloth and water — no abrasives.

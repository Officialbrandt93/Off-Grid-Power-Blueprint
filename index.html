Module 01 — Foundation
How off-grid solar works
Before buying anything, understand how the system works as a whole. Every component has one specific job — and they only function correctly when connected in the right order.

What "off-grid" means

An off-grid solar system generates and stores its own electricity — completely independent from the utility grid. There are no monthly bills, but also no grid as a backup. Every watt you use must be produced and stored by your own system, sized correctly from the start.

The system works 24 hours a day: panels capture energy during daylight, the battery stores it, and the inverter delivers it to your appliances day and night.

The solar panel is the starting point. Photovoltaic cells absorb photons and release electrons — the photovoltaic effect. The result is DC electricity. Panels are rated in watts and sized to meet your daily energy needs.

Module 02 — First Step
Know your energy needs
Before choosing any component, you need one number: how many watt-hours you consume per day. Every sizing calculation in this blueprint depends on it.

Why this is your first calculation

Watt-hours measure total energy, not power. A 100W bulb running for 3 hours consumes 300 Wh. Power times time equals energy.

Energy (Wh) = Power (W) x Quantity x Hours per day

How to find appliance wattage

Every appliance has a nameplate label on its back or bottom. Look for the number followed by "W". If the label shows a range like "50–80W", use the higher number. If unreadable, use a plug-in wattmeter.

Panasonic LED TV
ModelTH-40F201DX
Voltage110–240V
Frequency50/60 Hz
Power70 W ←
→
The wattage (70W here) is the only number you need. Multiply by daily hours of use to get Wh/day.

This TV running 3 hours/day:
70W × 3h = 210 Wh/day

Always add a 20–25% buffer on top of your total. Real usage is always slightly higher than calculated — appliances run longer, you add new devices, and losses accumulate.

What are peak sun hours?

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day when sunlight averages 1,000 W/m² — the standard at which panels are rated. It is not the same as daylight hours. Use globalsolaratlas.info to find your exact PSH. Always use a slightly conservative number — 10–15% below the annual average — to size for your worst winter months.

Module 05 — Control
Charge controllers
The charge controller sits between your panels and battery. Choose the wrong type and you could lose 20–30% of your panel potential every day.

What the charge controller does

The controller prevents overcharging and over-discharging. It also acts as a one-way gate, preventing the battery from discharging back through the panels at night.

PWM drags panel voltage down to battery voltage — 31W of potential is wasted as heat. MPPT tracks the panel optimal voltage and delivers the full rated power to the battery.
✓ Choose PWM when
  • Budget is very tight
  • System is under 200W
  • Panel voltage matches battery voltage
✓ Choose MPPT when
  • System is 200W or larger
  • Using 60-cell or 72-cell panels
  • Panel voltage is higher than battery voltage
  • You want maximum efficiency

Module 07 — Connections
Wiring, cables & safety
Undersized cables and missing fuses are the leading cause of off-grid system fires. This module ensures every wire and protective device is correctly rated.
Safety first. When in doubt, always size up — a slightly oversized cable or fuse costs a few dollars; a house fire does not.

Polarity — the rule that must never be broken

DC systems have polarity. Reversing polarity even briefly can instantly destroy your charge controller and inverter. Use color-coded cables throughout your entire installation.

Red cable — positive (+)
Panel +, battery +, controller + terminals
Black cable — negative (−)
Panel −, battery −, controller − terminals

Where fuses must be installed

NEC requires protection at three locations in every off-grid system. A fourth at the inverter AC output is strongly recommended.

1
Solar panels → charge controller
Protects the cable from panel short-circuit current. Place within 18 inches of the panels.
Fuse (A) = Panel Isc × 1.56 — round up to next standard size
2
Charge controller → battery bank
Protects the battery-side cable. Place as close to the battery as possible.
Fuse (A) = Controller max output current × 1.25
3
Battery bank → inverter (most critical)
The highest-current circuit. Must be within 18 inches of the battery terminal.
Fuse (A) = (Inverter W ÷ Battery V) × 1.56 — round up
4
Inverter AC output (recommended)
Protects the AC wiring going to your loads.
Breaker (A) = Inverter max AC output current × 1.25

Module 08 — Build
Installation & wiring order
Never connect components in random order. Follow this sequence every time — connecting the battery first can destroy components before you even notice a mistake.
Never connect the battery first. It energises all wiring immediately — any subsequent mistake with live cables can destroy components instantly.

Correct wiring sequence — click each step

1
Mount solar panels — do not connect yet
Install panels on the roof or mount. Leave MC4 connectors disconnected and cover panels with a dark cloth. A covered panel produces no current, making all subsequent wiring safe.
Why: Panels in sunlight are always live. Covering them ensures zero voltage at the cable ends during installation.
2
Mount and wire the charge controller
Fix the controller to the wall. Run cables from the controller to the battery location and to the panel location, but do not connect either end yet. Label all cables clearly.
Why: The controller needs to be physically in place before any connections are made.
3
Connect battery to charge controller
Connect positive first, then negative. The controller will power on and display battery voltage. Set your battery type and system voltage at this point.
Why: Most controllers require battery voltage to be detected before panels are connected, otherwise they may not initialise correctly.
4
Connect solar panels to charge controller
Uncover the panels and connect the MC4 panel cables to the controller PV input terminals. The controller will immediately begin charging if sunlight is available.
Why: Panels are connected after the battery so the controller already knows the target voltage before receiving panel input.
5
Connect inverter to battery bank
Run heavy-gauge cables from the battery to the inverter. Ensure the fuse is installed and the breaker is open (off). Connect positive first, then negative. Close the breaker to power the inverter.
Why: The inverter is connected last because it draws the most current and has the highest risk if something goes wrong.
6
Connect AC loads through inverter output
Wire your AC distribution panel or individual loads to the inverter AC output. Test each load one at a time. Verify voltage with a multimeter before connecting sensitive electronics.
Why: Testing loads one at a time makes it easy to identify any wiring issue without overwhelming the system on first startup.

Critical Reading
Safety Guide
Off-grid solar systems carry real electrical hazards. Read this page before touching any component. Most accidents are caused by skipping steps that take less than 60 seconds.

⚠ The rules that must never be broken

🔴
Battery last, battery first. Always connect the battery bank last when wiring up, and disconnect it first when doing maintenance. Every other component becomes live the moment the battery is connected.
🔴
Red = positive, black = negative. Always. Reversing polarity on a DC system instantly destroys charge controllers and inverters. There is no recovery — the component is dead. Use color-coded cables throughout with no exceptions.
🔴
Never work on live DC circuits. Unlike AC, DC arcs do not self-extinguish when a connection is broken. A DC short circuit sustains an arc flash that can cause severe burns and fires. Always disconnect and verify dead before working.
🔴
Fuse every circuit before energizing. A cable without a fuse is a potential fire starter. Never power up any part of the system without the required fuses installed at all 3 mandatory locations.
⚠️
Cover panels before disconnecting. A solar panel in sunlight is always live — even with the controller disconnected. Cover panels with a dark cloth or thick cardboard before working on panel cables.
⚠️
Never charge lithium below 0°C. Charging a LiFePO4 battery below freezing causes permanent internal damage called lithium plating. Use a temperature sensor connected to the charge controller to prevent this automatically.

Personal protective equipment (PPE)

Wear the correct PPE whenever working on or near live circuits. These items cost very little compared to the injuries they prevent.

Insulated gloves
Rated 1000V minimum. Wear whenever handling live terminals.
Safety glasses
Protects from arc flash sparks and battery acid splatter.
Insulated tools
Screwdrivers and pliers rated 1000V. Never use uninsulated tools near live terminals.
Multimeter
Verify dead before touching. Always measure voltage before every connection.

Battery storage and ventilation

Where and how you store your batteries affects both safety and battery lifespan.

⚠️
FLA batteries release hydrogen gas during charging. Hydrogen is explosive at concentrations above 4%. Never store flooded lead-acid batteries in enclosed spaces, near open flames, or in living areas. Install a vent directly to the outside.

Emergency procedures

🔥 Electrical fire
  1. Do NOT use water — it conducts electricity
  2. Use a Class C or CO₂ extinguisher only
  3. Disconnect the battery if safe to do so
  4. Evacuate and call emergency services
  5. Do not re-enter until cleared by professionals
⚡ Electric shock
  1. Do NOT touch the person directly
  2. Disconnect power at the source first
  3. Use a non-conductive object to separate if needed
  4. Call emergency services immediately
  5. Begin CPR if trained and if person is unresponsive
💥 Short circuit / arc
  1. Disconnect battery immediately if safe
  2. Do not touch the arcing point — extreme heat
  3. If fire starts, treat as electrical fire
  4. Inspect all cables before re-energizing
  5. Replace any melted or damaged cables before restart

Legal and code requirements

📄
Check local regulations before installing. In many countries and states, connecting any solar system to a household distribution panel requires a licensed electrician. A fully standalone off-grid system (not connected to the grid or main panel) is generally DIY-legal in most jurisdictions, but local codes vary. Always verify before starting work.
📄
The NEC formulas in this guide are a starting point, not a substitute for professional review. For large systems (over 5kW), systems in commercial properties, or any system connected to a distribution panel, consult a licensed solar installer or electrician.
